# CRITIQUE + REDESIGN — HooknLoop 60s
### From "collection of clips" to "one continuous story"

---

# PART 1 — CRITIQUE OF THE CURRENT ROUGH CUT

**Verdict: the footage is competent; the film is not.** Individual frames would pass as real photography. Assembled, they read as a taxonomy of industries rather than a commercial. Below, honestly, per dimension.

### Storytelling — **2/10. The core failure.**
There is no story. There is a **list**. The structure is literally "here is a vertical, here is another vertical" — upholstery, signage, cable, classroom, caravan, warehouse, defence, school, packaging, automotive, warehousing. Eleven sectors, no causality, no protagonist, no change of state. Nothing that happens in shot 4 is *because* of shot 3. You can shuffle the middle 40 seconds into any order and lose nothing — that is the definition of a montage without a spine. A catalogue is not a narrative.

### Pacing / editing rhythm — **3/10.**
Shots run 3.0–3.5s each in a near-metronomic sequence. That regularity is exactly what makes it feel like a slide deck — a PowerPoint advances at a constant interval; a film does not. There is no accelerando into the emotional peak, no held beat, no rest. The one long shot (the 8s aged-care hold) doesn't read as a climax because nothing built toward it.

### Product visibility — **2/10. Commercially disqualifying.**
The product is a small dark strip, usually partly occluded by hands, never in hero focus, never with branded packaging in frame. A viewer who doesn't already know what hook-and-loop tape is will not learn it here. **We are selling a product that is never actually shown as a product.** No roll, no packaging, no clear "this is the thing you buy."

### Branding — **2/10.**
57 seconds of unbranded footage, then a logo card. The truck is the only branded element and it's front-loaded then abandoned. Brand recall from this structure is near zero. Swap the end card and this sells any fastening brand on earth — which fails the brief's own kill-test.

### Continuity — **3/10.**
Different hands, different people, different lighting temperature and time-of-day in every shot. Overcast defence shot cuts to golden-hour strap cuts to cool industrial warehouse. No character to follow, no prop that recurs, no consistent sun position. The film has no *place* and no *day*.

### Transitions — **1/10.**
Every cut is a hard cut on nothing. The original treatment specified motivated match-cuts (shape, movement, colour, the rip itself). None survived into the build. Unmotivated cuts between unrelated subjects are precisely what makes AI montages feel arbitrary.

### Cinematography — **5/10.**
Individually decent. Collectively monotonous: almost every shot is a medium-close-up of hands, centred, at roughly the same distance. No wides to establish scale, no true macro to reveal the mechanism, no variety in height or angle. One visual grammar repeated 15 times.

### Sound design — **0/10.**
Nonexistent. This is the single biggest missed opportunity in the entire project. HooknLoop owns one of the most recognisable sounds in the physical world — the **RIP** — and the current cut is silent apart from VO. The original brief correctly identified this sound as the brand's signature and the film's percussion. Discarding it throws away the most ownable asset available.

### Narration — **3/10.**
Abstract poetry ("the temporary, the permanent") laid over literal industrial footage. The register of the words and the register of the pictures don't match. Coverage was also far too sparse in the first build (13.6s of speech across 60s), leaving dead air that reads as broken rather than restrained.

### Music — **0/10.** Absent from the delivered cut.

### Lighting / realism / colour — **6/10.**
The strongest dimension. Warm, natural, no teal-orange, believable falloff. Undermined by inconsistency between shots rather than by quality within them.

### Emotional impact — **2/10.**
The aged-care shot is the only moment with genuine feeling, and it's orphaned — it arrives after nine unrelated industrial shots and is followed by a logo. Emotion requires setup; there is none.

# PART 2 — WHY IT FEELS AI-GENERATED

Not because the pixels look fake. Because of **structural** tells:

1. **Every shot is a self-contained island.** Real productions reuse locations, props, people, and light because shooting is expensive. AI montages don't, because generating a new world is as cheap as reusing one. The *absence of reuse* is the giveaway.
2. **No continuity of person.** Fifteen shots, fifteen different sets of hands. No human production works this way.
3. **Uniform shot duration.** Human editors cut on feeling, action, and music. Constant intervals signal automated assembly.
4. **Ken Burns on stills.** Zero parallax, mathematically linear zoom. Instantly readable as a still, not footage.
5. **Everything is too clean.** No cable slack, no scuffed benches, no half-drunk coffee, no mess. Real workplaces are cluttered; these are showrooms.
6. **Perfect centred composition** in nearly every frame. No accidental framing, no lead-room asymmetry, no operator drift.
7. **No camera imperfection.** No breathing, no focus hunt, no micro-shake, no lens flare artefact. Sterile stability.
8. **Unmotivated cuts.** Nothing in the outgoing frame invites the incoming frame.
9. **Lighting discontinuity** between adjacent shots implies impossible geography and time.

**The fix is not higher-fidelity generation. It is narrative causality, prop/character continuity, motivated transitions, deliberate imperfection, and sound.**

# PART 3 — THE REDESIGN

## The idea

> ## "EVERYTHING HOLDS"
> **We follow one roll of HooknLoop tape through a single Australian day** — off the warehouse shelf at dawn, onto the truck, down the highway, and into the hands of the trades who depend on it. The product is the protagonist. The day is the structure. The rip is the rhythm.

**Why this fixes the problem:** it supplies the three things the current cut lacks — *causality* (the roll travels, so each scene is consequent on the last), *continuity* (one day, one sun arc, one product line recurring in frame), and *product centrality* (the thing we sell is literally the main character, visible and branded throughout, not an incidental dark strip).

It also earns the truck. Currently the truck is a bolted-on opener. Here it is the **connective tissue** of the second act — the reason the product gets from the shelf to the site — which is exactly what a logistics-proud Australian supplier should be dramatising.

## Tone references
Volvo Trucks (industrial dignity, real machinery) · Toyota Australia (landscape + trade authenticity) · Apple (restraint, product as hero, negative space) · Patagonia (unglamorised competence).

# PART 6 — SOUND DESIGN MAP

**The RIP is the brand.** It is the percussion line of the entire film.

| Layer | Content |
|---|---|
| **Signature** | RIP (slow peel, fast rip, small strip, wide strap) · PRESS (soft click, firm press, industrial thud) |
| **Rhythm** | Act III presses land on the musical beat; Act IV montage is cut to rip/press percussion |
| **Ambience** | warehouse reverb · magpies + distant highway (dawn) · site clatter · workshop compressor · caravan-park birds · studio quiet · highway tyre roar |
| **Foley** | boots on gravel, cardboard, cable rustle, fabric tension, roller door, truck door, engine |
| **Space** | real reverb per environment — warehouse long, caravan tight, studio dead |
| **Silence** | full drop at 0:48 (product beauty) and after the final PRESS — silence is the frame around the logo |

# PART 7 — MUSIC DIRECTION

Modern cinematic, organic, never corporate. **Builds in three stages:**
1. **0:00–0:11** — near silence. A single felt-piano note, room tone, sub-bass warmth. Let the world breathe.
2. **0:11–0:42** — organic percussion enters (hand-struck wood, brushed textures, low kick), locked to the rip/press beats. Warm piano motif establishes. Light strings under.
3. **0:42–0:56** — strings open, motif resolves major, gentle emotional lift. **Not** a trailer swell — restraint.
4. **0:56–0:60** — everything drops. One clean PRESS. Logo. Silence.

Avoid: epic trailer drums, orchestral hits, corporate-uplift piano loops, EDM builds, anything with vocals.
